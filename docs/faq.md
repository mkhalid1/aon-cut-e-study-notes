# FAQ

The first group of questions comes from Google's "People also ask" box and related searches for Aon assessment queries (pulled 24 November 2026, see [Sources](sources.md#search-data)). The rest are questions candidates ask about the format. Facts are from Aon's own pages; the advice is ours.

## What is an Aon assessment test?

A set of short online tests and questionnaires that an employer asks you to complete during recruitment, delivered by Aon. Aon bought the assessment company cut-e in 2017, which is why the same tests are also called cut-e tests. Which tests you get depends on the employer and the role. These notes cover three of them in depth: scales numerical, scales verbal and scales ix. See the [test overview](test-overview.md).

## How do I prepare for an Aon assessment?

Find out which tests you were sent, take the [diagnostic](diagnostic.md), learn the rules of each format from the section guides, then do at least one timed run. Aon's own advice is to watch its demo videos and get familiar with how the tests work. The [study plans](study-plans.md) lay this out over 7 days or 2 days.

## How does Aon detect cheating?

Aon says employers do not tolerate someone else doing the test for you, and that retests are often used: if you reach an interview, you may be asked to repeat a module from the online assessment. A result you could not reproduce would show up then. Aon does not publish any other detail on how it checks.

## Is there a free Aon practice test?

Aon publishes free practice task documents and demo videos on its [candidate preparation page](https://www.aon.com/en/capabilities/talent-and-rewards/prepare-for-your-online-assessment), each with five worked examples. For a timed run, the [free Aon cut-e style practice test on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/aon-cut-e?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=study-notes&utm_content=aon-cut-e) covers numerical, verbal and inductive items with explanations. This repo has 45 original practice items.

## Where can I find Aon test answers?

There are no answer sheets to the real tests that you could use, and searching for them wastes prep time. Each test draws on its own data and figures. What does transfer is the method: the rules for TRUE, FALSE and CANNOT SAY, and the rule families in scales ix. Those are in the section guides.

## What is the Aon numerical reasoning test?

Aon's scales numerical: 37 statements in 12 minutes about tabs of tables and charts, each answered TRUE, FALSE or CANNOT SAY. See the [numerical guide](sections/scales-numerical.md).

## What is the Aon verbal reasoning test?

Aon's scales verbal: 49 statements in 12 minutes about tabs of text, each answered TRUE, FALSE or CANNOT SAY. See the [verbal guide](sections/scales-verbal.md).

## How are Aon test results scored?

Aon says ability tests usually add points for correct answers and deduct points for incorrect ones, then calculate the result at the end. Aon does not publish a pass mark. See [How scoring works](scoring.md).

## Is there negative marking on the Aon tests?

Aon says points are usually deducted for incorrect answers, without giving the size of the deduction. In practice: answer what you can justify, and do not click at random in the last minute.

## How long do the tests take?

scales numerical 12 minutes, scales verbal 12 minutes, scales ix 5 minutes. Aon says most single tests last under fifteen minutes and most processes have no more than five tests. The untimed instructions and examples come on top.

## Can I use a calculator?

For scales numerical, yes: Aon tells candidates to have a pen, paper and calculator to hand. Aon says that if any aid is needed or helpful for a test, the test's introduction will tell you.

## Can I take the tests on my phone?

Aon says almost all its tests work on smartphones and tablets, but strongly recommends a PC or laptop with an up-to-date browser and a stable connection. For tab-based tests like numerical and verbal, a bigger screen helps.

## Can I take breaks?

Between tests, yes. Aon says you do not have to take all tests in one go and usually have several days. Within a test, no: once a test starts, it cannot be paused.

## What happens if my test crashes?

Aon says this is rare and not a catastrophe: contact the company that invited you, and they can unblock the test.

## Can I retake an Aon test?

Aon does not publish a retake policy on its candidate page. It depends on the employer. Ask the recruiter.

## Do I need to finish every question?

No. Aon says it is normal not to answer all questions in the time. Aim for as many correct answers as you can, at a careful pace.
