# The Aon cut-e scales tests explained

If an employer has sent you an "Aon assessment" or a "cut-e test", you are likely facing one or more short, separately timed tests from Aon's scales family. These notes cover three of them in depth: **scales numerical, scales verbal and scales ix**.

## Quick facts

| | scales numerical | scales verbal | scales ix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aon's name | Numerical Reasoning | Verbal Reasoning | Inductive Reasoning |
| Length | 12 minutes | 12 minutes | 5 minutes |
| Tasks | 37 | 49 | 20 |
| You see | Tabs of tables and charts | Tabs of text | Nine numbered figures |
| You answer | TRUE, FALSE or CANNOT SAY | TRUE, FALSE or CANNOT SAY | The figure that breaks the rule |
| Expected to finish? | No | No | No |

Source for every row: [Aon, Prepare for your Online Assessment](https://www.aon.com/en/capabilities/talent-and-rewards/prepare-for-your-online-assessment) and Aon's practice task documents, checked 24 November 2026. Full list in [Sources](sources.md).

## Why it is called both Aon and cut-e

cut-e was an online assessment company based in Hamburg, founded in 2002. Aon announced that it had acquired cut-e on 4 May 2017. Aon's own candidate pages now describe its assessment business as "Aon's Assessment Solutions (formerly cut-e)". So "Aon test", "cut-e test" and "scales test" usually mean the same family of tests. "scales" is the name Aon gives to many of the individual tests, such as scales numerical and scales ix.

## How each test session runs

Aon describes the same structure for all its tests:

1. **A short introduction** describing the task.
2. **An interactive walkthrough** showing how to answer. You usually have to perform an action to move on.
3. **Example items, with no time limit.** Aon says you can take these as many times as you like. Use them to settle in.
4. **A summary page** with the key points.
5. **The timed test.** Once it starts, you cannot pause it. When time is up, it ends automatically.

You normally take each test separately and can take breaks between tests. You usually have several days to finish them all, and Aon says the total time you take across the tests is not monitored. Check the deadline in your invitation.

## What each test is really checking

- **scales numerical:** can you find the right figure in a set of tables and charts, respect its units, and tell when the data simply does not answer the question? See the [numerical guide](sections/scales-numerical.md).
- **scales verbal:** can you match a statement to a text by meaning, spot a contradiction, and refuse to assume what is not written? See the [verbal guide](sections/scales-verbal.md).
- **scales ix:** can you work out an unstated rule from examples and find the case that breaks it, quickly? See the [ix guide](sections/scales-ix.md).

## The one rule that runs through all three

**Only what is on the screen counts.** The numerical and verbal tests reward you for saying CANNOT SAY when the tabs do not settle a claim, however likely it sounds. The ix test rewards you for following the rule the figures show, not the figure that looks strangest. Most lost marks come from adding something that is not there.

## Speed, accuracy and wrong answers

All three tests give you more items than most people can finish, and Aon says that is normal. Aon also says that in ability tests points are usually added for correct answers and **deducted for incorrect answers**, and advises working "quickly but carefully", no faster than you would at school or university. Aon does not publish the size of the deduction. See [How scoring works](scoring.md) for what that means in practice.
