# Study plans

Two plans: one for a week, one for when the invitation has already arrived. Both start with the [15-minute diagnostic](diagnostic.md). First, check your invitation: if you were sent only one or two of the three tests, drop the days for the others and spend the time on what you will actually sit.

Expect about 45 to 75 minutes a day on the 7-day plan.

## 7-day plan

| Day | Focus | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Baseline | Read the [test overview](test-overview.md) and [scoring](scoring.md). Take the [diagnostic](diagnostic.md). Note your weakest test. |
| 2 | Numerical | Read the [numerical guide](sections/scales-numerical.md). Do all 5 items untimed. For each miss, write which tab you used and what you missed. |
| 3 | Verbal | Read the [verbal guide](sections/scales-verbal.md). Do all 5 items. For each TRUE or FALSE, write the sentence that decides it. |
| 4 | ix | Read the [ix guide](sections/scales-ix.md). Do all 5 items. For each, write the rule as one sentence before checking. |
| 5 | First timed run | Take a timed practice test, such as the [free one on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/aon-cut-e?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=study-notes&utm_content=aon-cut-e). Read [timing and pacing](timing.md). Note where you ran out of time. |
| 6 | Fix the weakest test | Re-read that guide's traps list. Redo its 5 items against a timer: 100 seconds for numerical, 75 for verbal, 75 for ix. Find fresh practice for that format. |
| 7 | Second timed run, then rest | Take a second timed run in the morning. Compare with day 5. Read the [test-day checklist](test-day-checklist.md). Stop by early evening. |

### How to review a missed item

For each miss, answer three questions:

1. **Which rule did I break?** For example: ignored the units, assumed a cause, counted wrong, picked the strangest-looking figure.
2. **What on the screen should have stopped me?** Name the word, number or figure.
3. **What will I check next time?** One short habit, such as "say the unit out loud".

Keep these in one list. By day 6 the same two or three mistakes will keep showing up. Fixing those is worth more than any amount of new practice.

## 2-day plan

For when the invitation is in and the deadline is close. Aon says you usually have several days to complete all the tests; check your own deadline.

### Day 1: learn the rules

| Block | Time | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 15 min | Take the [diagnostic](diagnostic.md). |
| 2 | 20 min | Read the "How the questions look" and "Traps" parts of each guide you need. |
| 3 | 40 min | Do the practice items for your weakest test. Read every explanation. |
| 4 | 20 min | Take a timed practice run, such as the [free one on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/aon-cut-e?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=study-notes&utm_content=aon-cut-e). |

### Day 2: sharpen, then sit the tests

| Block | Time | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 30 min | Do the practice items for your other tests. |
| 2 | 10 min | Read [timing and pacing](timing.md) and write your checkpoints on paper. |
| 3 | 10 min | Read the [test-day checklist](test-day-checklist.md). |
| 4 | | Take the real tests when you are fresh, one at a time, with a break between them. |

## What not to do

- **Do not try to memorise items.** The real tests use their own data and figures. Learn the rules and the method.
- **Do not practise only untimed.** You need at least one timed run per test you will sit.
- **Do not take all the real tests in one tired evening.** Aon lets you split them up. Use that.
- **Do not chase a rumoured pass mark.** Aon does not publish one. Aim to get as many right as you can without careless errors.
