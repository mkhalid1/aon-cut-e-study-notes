# Timing and pacing

All three scales tests give you more items than you can comfortably finish. Pacing is not about reaching the end. It is about how many correct answers you bank, without throwing marks away on wrong ones.

## The budget

| Test | Time | Tasks (Aon) | Average if you tried to answer all |
|---|---|---|---|
| scales numerical | 12 minutes | 37 | about 19 seconds |
| scales verbal | 12 minutes | 49 | about 15 seconds |
| scales ix | 5 minutes | 20 | 15 seconds |

The averages are simple arithmetic on Aon's published figures. Aon says it is normal not to finish.

## A pacing plan for each test

**scales numerical (12 minutes)**

- **0:00 to 0:30:** read the tab titles and units. Do not skip this.
- **Then:** answer lookups in 10 to 15 seconds, calculations in up to 40.
- **At 6:00:** glance at your count. Around 15 or more answered with care is a reasonable pace.
- **Last minute:** keep checking units. Do not switch into guessing.

**scales verbal (12 minutes)**

- **0:00 to 0:30:** skim the tab titles so you know where each topic lives.
- **Then:** 10 to 15 seconds for one-sentence checks, up to 30 seconds for two-sentence ones.
- **At 6:00:** around 20 or more answered is a reasonable pace.
- **Torn between TRUE and CANNOT SAY?** If you cannot point to the sentence, choose CANNOT SAY.

**scales ix (5 minutes)**

- **First 2 minutes:** the early items are the easiest. Aim for 6 to 8 done.
- **Middle:** up to about 25 seconds each.
- **Any item:** if you cannot name a rule in about 30 seconds, move on.

These checkpoints are our own guidance, not Aon's.

## Where time usually goes

- **Re-finding tabs.** People who skip the opening skim go hunting for the right tab on every statement. Thirty seconds at the start saves minutes.
- **Calculating what you could estimate.** "More than double" or "more than half" can usually be settled by rounding.
- **Re-reading a verbal tab from the top.** Go to the sentence that matches the statement's key word instead.
- **Staring at one ix item.** A late item can eat a minute and earn nothing. The next one may take ten seconds.

## Practise against the clock

Untimed practice builds accuracy. Timed practice builds pace. Once your accuracy on the section guides is good, do at least two timed runs. The [free Aon cut-e style practice test on PrepClubs](https://prepclubs.com/free-practice-tests/aon-cut-e?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=study-notes&utm_content=aon-cut-e) runs all three formats against one clock, so you can practise switching between them.
