# scales numerical

**In one line:** you get a few tabs of tables and charts about one organisation, then a run of statements, and you mark each one TRUE, FALSE or CANNOT SAY.

Aon lists this test as Numerical Reasoning (scales numerical): **37 tasks in 12 minutes, finish as many as you can.** That is under 20 seconds per statement if you tried to answer them all, and Aon says plainly that you are not expected to. The skill being tested is finding the right number fast and checking a claim against it, more than heavy maths.

## What it measures

Aon describes it as your ability to analyse and understand numerical information and judge whether statements are true on the basis of the data given. In practice that splits into three habits:

- **Locating:** going straight to the right tab, row and column.
- **Reading units:** thousands versus millions, per cent versus count, one year versus a total.
- **Knowing the limits of the data:** recognising when a claim needs a number the tabs never give.

The third habit is the one that separates scores. Most wrong answers on this test are not arithmetic slips. They are a CANNOT SAY statement answered TRUE or FALSE because it felt obvious.

## How the questions look

The screen has a set of data sheets, usually shown as tabs, all about one fictional company. The sheets do not change during the test. Statements appear one at a time. Aon states that **each statement relates to only one data sheet**, so you never have to combine numbers from two tabs.

| Answer | Choose it when |
|---|---|
| **TRUE** | The data on the sheet shows the statement is correct. |
| **FALSE** | The data on the sheet shows the statement is wrong. A number that is close but not equal counts as wrong, unless the statement itself says "about" or "roughly". |
| **CANNOT SAY** | The sheet does not hold the information needed to decide. |

Aon's own worked examples make two points worth remembering. A table headed "in thousands" means 7,256 is 7.256 million, and a statement that misses this is wrong. And when a statement claims an exact figure, being close is not enough: 1,550,000 is not 1,500,000.

Aon tells candidates to have a pen, paper and a calculator ready before the test starts.

## A method that works

1. **Spend your first 30 seconds on the tabs, not the first statement.** Read each tab's title, its units and its time span. You will come back to these tabs dozens of times, so a quick map pays for itself.
2. **Read the statement and name its tab.** Every statement has a topic word (vans, complaints, regions, quarters). Match it to one tab. If no tab covers that topic at all, the answer is CANNOT SAY.
3. **Find the exact cells.** Row, column, year, unit. Say the unit to yourself: "thousand parcels", "per cent of complaints".
4. **Ask: does the sheet hold everything the claim needs?** Counts from percentages need a total. Per-quarter volumes need volumes, not rates. Reasons need a stated reason. If a piece is missing, it is CANNOT SAY, however likely the claim sounds.
5. **Estimate first, then calculate only if it is close.** If the claim says "more than doubled" and the numbers go from 198 to 363, you can see 363 is short of 396 without a calculator. Only reach for the calculator when the estimate lands near the claim.
6. **Check the wording one last time.** "Every year", "only", "more than", "at least", "the same percentage". One word decides many items.

## Traps that cost points

- **Missing the unit line.** "Thousands" under a table header turns 1,622 into 1.622 million. Statements are written to catch people who skip it.
- **Treating rates as counts.** A higher on-time percentage in one quarter does not mean more parcels arrived on time, because the quarters may handle different volumes.
- **Answering from real-world sense.** Fewer parcels does not prove fewer customers. If the sheet does not measure customers, the answer is CANNOT SAY.
- **Calling a near miss TRUE.** "Grew by 20%" is false if the growth was 18%. Exact claims need exact matches.
- **Mixing up percentage points and per cent.** Moving from 10% to 15% is a rise of 5 percentage points but a 50% increase.
- **Using two tabs.** Aon says each statement needs one sheet. If you find yourself combining two, re-read the statement: you have probably picked the wrong sheet.
- **Assuming a percentage of a changing total.** A region's share of the total can fall even while its own numbers rise, if other regions grow faster.

## Timing

Twelve minutes for 37 statements is about 19 seconds each. Most people answer well short of 37, and that is normal: Aon says it is normal not to finish. Aon also says points are usually added for each correct answer and deducted for each incorrect one, so a fast wrong answer can cost you more than a skipped one. As guidance:

- Answer quick lookups in 10 to 15 seconds to bank time.
- Give a calculation up to about 40 seconds.
- If you have no idea after that, do not stab at it. A careful CANNOT SAY check usually takes seconds, so do that check before guessing.

## Practice items

These 5 statements are original, written for these notes, and not taken from Aon or any prep site. All 5 use the four tabs below, the way the real test reuses one set of tabs. Each statement uses one tab only.

**Harlow Freight: data sheets**

**Tab 1. Parcels delivered by region (thousands)**

| Region | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| North | 420 | 462 | 508 |
| South | 610 | 640 | 608 |
| East | 275 | 330 | 363 |
| West | 195 | 190 | 228 |

**Tab 2. Delivery fleet at year end**

| | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vans in fleet | 1,200 | 1,320 | 1,452 |
| Share of vans that are electric | 10% | 15% | 25% |

**Tab 3. Parcels delivered on time, 2025 (% of that quarter's parcels)**

| Q1 | Q2 | Q3 | Q4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 91% | 94% | 96% | 88% |

**Tab 4. Customer complaints in 2025 by main reason (total: 2,400 complaints)**

| Late delivery | Damaged parcel | Wrong address | Other |
|---|---|---|---|
| 40% | 25% | 20% | 15% |

### Statements 1 to 5

**Item 1.** The number of parcels delivered in the East rose by 20% from 2023 to 2024.

**Item 2.** The West region lost customers between 2023 and 2024.

**Item 3.** The number of electric vans more than doubled from 2024 to 2025.

**Item 4.** More parcels were delivered on time in Q3 2025 than in Q2 2025.

**Item 5.** In 2025, complaints about damaged parcels and wrong addresses together outnumbered complaints about late delivery.

<details>
<summary>Answers and explanations, items 1 to 5</summary>

**Item 1: TRUE.** Tab 1. East went from 275 to 330 thousand, a rise of 55. 55 divided by 275 is exactly 0.2, so 20%. *Why not FALSE:* the statement gives an exact figure, and it matches exactly.

**Item 2: CANNOT SAY.** Tab 1. West parcels fell from 195 to 190 thousand, but the tab counts parcels, not customers. The same customers could simply have sent fewer parcels. *Why not TRUE:* fewer parcels and fewer customers are different measures, and no tab counts customers.

**Item 3: FALSE.** Tab 2. Electric vans in 2024: 15% of 1,320 = 198. In 2025: 25% of 1,452 = 363. Doubling 198 gives 396, and 363 is below that. *Why not TRUE:* the share went from 15% to 25%, which feels like a big jump, but the count rose by about 83%, not 100%.

**Item 4: CANNOT SAY.** Tab 3 gives percentages of each quarter's own parcels, not numbers of parcels. If Q2 handled far more parcels than Q3, 94% of Q2 could be more parcels than 96% of Q3. The sheet gives no quarterly volumes. *Why not TRUE:* a higher rate is not a higher count.

**Item 5: TRUE.** Tab 4. Damaged plus wrong address is 25% + 20% = 45% of complaints, against 40% for late delivery. Because all three are shares of the same 2,400, comparing the percentages is enough. *Why not CANNOT SAY:* no extra figure is needed when the shares come from one total.

</details>

## Score yourself

| Item | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Answer | T | CS | F | CS | T |

- **5 of 5:** your reading of data is sound. Work on speed: redo the set against a 100-second timer.
- **3 to 4:** check which kind you missed. If it was CANNOT SAY items (2, 4), go back to method step 4. If it was the calculation item (3), practise estimating before calculating.
- **2 or fewer:** slow down and say the unit and the tab out loud for every statement for a few days. Accuracy first, speed later.
