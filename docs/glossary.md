# Glossary

**Aon Assessment Solutions.** The part of Aon that delivers online talent assessments. Aon describes it as formerly cut-e.

**CANNOT SAY.** The answer in scales numerical and scales verbal when the data sheet neither confirms nor contradicts a statement.

**cut-e.** The Hamburg-based assessment company, founded in 2002, that Aon acquired in 2017. Many people still call the tests "cut-e tests".

**Data sheet.** One tab of information in scales numerical (tables and charts) or scales verbal (text). Each statement relates to one data sheet.

**Deduction.** Points taken away for an incorrect answer. Aon says ability tests usually deduct points for wrong answers.

**Distractor.** In scales ix, a feature that changes at random to pull your attention away from the real rule.

**Example items.** The untimed practice items at the start of each Aon test. You can repeat them as often as you like.

**FALSE.** The answer when the data sheet contradicts the statement. In scales numerical, a near miss on an exact figure is FALSE.

**Inductive reasoning.** Working out a general rule from examples. scales ix tests it.

**Odd one out.** The scales ix format: one of nine figures breaks the rule the others follow.

**Paraphrase.** A statement that says the same thing as the text in different words. In scales verbal, a fair paraphrase is TRUE.

**Percentage point.** The difference between two percentages. 10% to 15% is 5 percentage points, but a 50% increase.

**Retest.** Repeating a module later in the process, often at interview, to confirm your online result.

**scales.** The family name Aon gives to many of its tests, written in lower case: scales numerical, scales verbal, scales ix and others.

**scales ix.** Aon's Inductive Reasoning test: 20 tasks in 5 minutes, find the one figure of nine that breaks the rule.

**scales numerical.** Aon's Numerical Reasoning test: 37 tasks in 12 minutes, TRUE, FALSE or CANNOT SAY.

**scales verbal.** Aon's Verbal Reasoning test: 49 tasks in 12 minutes, TRUE, FALSE or CANNOT SAY.

**Sequence rule.** In scales ix, a rule where the figures change step by step from 1 to 9.

**Shared-property rule.** In scales ix, a rule where every figure has something in common, whatever the order.

**TRUE.** The answer when the data sheet shows the statement is correct, including by paraphrase.

**Unit line.** The note on a table or chart that says what the numbers mean, such as "thousands". Missing it is a classic numerical trap.
