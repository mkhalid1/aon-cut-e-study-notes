# How scoring works

Short version: Aon says ability tests usually add points for correct answers and take points away for incorrect ones, the result is calculated at the end, and the employer decides what result it needs. Aon does not publish a pass mark or the exact deduction.

## What Aon says about scoring

From Aon's candidate page, in its own words paraphrased:

- **Points per answer.** For performance (ability) tests, points are usually added for each correct answer and deducted for each incorrect answer. The final result is calculated at the end of the test.
- **Not finishing is normal.** The time limits exist because without them everyone would score the same. It is normal not to answer every question.
- **Do not rush.** Aon advises working quickly but carefully, and no faster than you did at school or in your studies, because incorrect answers generally hurt the final result.
- **Feedback report.** Each test has a feedback report explaining the result. Whether you see it is up to the employer.

Source: [Aon, Prepare for your Online Assessment](https://www.aon.com/en/capabilities/talent-and-rewards/prepare-for-your-online-assessment), checked 24 November 2026.

## What this means for you

A deduction for wrong answers changes the usual test advice.

| Usual advice | On a scales test |
|---|---|
| Never leave a question blank. | A blind guess can cost you. Answer what you can justify. |
| Rush the last minute to answer everything. | Keep the same care to the end. A burst of wrong answers can undo earlier work. |
| Speed is everything. | Accuracy and speed both count. Many correct answers at a steady pace beats many answers with lots of errors. |

There is a middle ground. If you have narrowed a numerical or verbal statement to two options and have a reason to prefer one, answer it: an informed choice is not a blind guess. On scales ix, if you have a rule that fits eight figures, answer even if you are not fully sure. What to avoid is clicking with no reason at all.

## What Aon does not publish

We looked for these on Aon's own pages and did not find them:

- **A pass mark.** Aon gives none for any scales test. Any threshold is the employer's decision.
- **The deduction per wrong answer.** Aon says points are usually deducted, not how many.
- **The comparison group.** Aon's candidate page does not say whether, or against whom, your result is compared with other candidates.

If anyone quotes you a precise pass mark for scales numerical or scales ix, ask where it comes from. It is not on Aon's candidate pages.

## Retests and honesty

Aon says employers do not tolerate someone else completing a test for you, and that retests are often run later in the process: if you reach an interview, you may be asked to repeat a module. A score you could not repeat will show up then. Prepare so that your own result is one you can match on the day.

## Your data

Aon says employers should tell you how your data is handled, that Aon recommends results are kept for a time and then deleted, and that as a rule data in Aon's systems is deleted after 12 months. You can ask the employer to delete your data at any time.
